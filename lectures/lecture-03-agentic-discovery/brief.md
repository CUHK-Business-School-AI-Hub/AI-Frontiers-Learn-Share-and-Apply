## Abstract

LLM agents are moving from short tool-using tasks to long-horizon, open-ended work such as scientific research. This talk presents three parts of our work along this path. First, I will give an overview of our research on agents, from LLM-powered data operations to data agents and learning from verified discovery. Second, I will introduce AgentScope, our open-source framework for building, deploying, and tuning agents, built around an agent loop and a harness for tools, context, memory, and sandboxed execution. Third, I will present InfinityScope, an agentic workspace for scientists built with AgentScope. In InfinityScope, a scientist works with a team of agents to grow a reasoning graph of claims via reasoning (with LLMs) and experiments (with tools), each attempted by agents and checked by verifier agents, so the scientist can explore many lines of attack in parallel, steer the agents through conversation, and receive a verified report of what has been established and what remains open.

## Speaker biography

Dr. Bolin Ding is a Researcher and Senior Director of Research at Alibaba Token Hub. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign, his M.Phil. in Systems Engineering and Engineering Management from The Chinese University of Hong Kong, and his B.S. in Mathematics and Applied Mathematics from Renmin University of China. His research aims to make systems intelligent and efficient with machine learning and optimization techniques. His recent work focuses on infrastructure for building, deploying, and tuning LLM agents, efficient data pipelines for training LLMs and agent models, and novel agent applications in AI for data analytics and AI for science. His research has been extensively deployed in production and has grown into widely adopted open-source projects such as AgentScope and Data-Juicer. Before joining Alibaba, he was a researcher at Microsoft Research, working on database systems and data privacy. He publishes in top conferences and journals in related areas, including SIGMOD, VLDB, ICDE, KDD, ICML, NeurIPS, ICLR, and SODA.
